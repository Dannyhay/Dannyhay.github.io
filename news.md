      <p><strong><em>ESSD</em> highlight paper, with a video abstract.</strong> <em>The Standardized Soil Moisture–Temperature Compound Index (SSTCI): daily scale global data set and event catalogue for compound dry–hot extremes (1961–2023)</em>, led by Rukhshinda Aftab, is published in <em>Earth System Science Data</em> (18, 7981–8002; <a href="https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-7981-2026">doi:10.5194/essd-18-7981-2026</a>) and has been selected by the editors as a <strong>highlight paper</strong>. It delivers two linked products for global land at 0.1° resolution: a continuous daily severity index built from soil-moisture memory and surface air temperature, and a ready-to-use catalogue recording when and where each compound dry–hot event occurred, how long it lasted and how severe it was. Both show these events becoming more frequent and longer-lasting in many regions over recent decades. Data: <a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18280747">SSTCI</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20826759">CDHE event catalogue</a>.</p>

    <p><strong>New paper in <em>Earth System Dynamics</em>.</strong> <em>Benchmarking conditioners in Liang–Kleeman Information Flow: application to land–atmosphere interactions in humid forests</em>, led by Fareeha Siddique with David Docquier and Stéphane Vannitsem, is out (<em>Earth Syst. Dynam.</em> 17, 1551–1572; <a href="https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-17-1551-2026">doi:10.5194/esd-17-1551-2026</a>). We test what happens to causal estimates when mediating and confounding variables are added to the information-flow framework. In synthetic tests the correct causal directions survive even hidden confounding; in the humid forests of south-east China and the Amazon, the soil moisture–leaf area link stays stable, while the soil moisture–productivity and vapour-pressure-deficit links diverge most under compound dry–hot conditions.</p>

    <p><strong>Human heat stress across the Arabian Peninsula in the <em>International Journal of Climatology</em>.</strong> <em>Assessment of human thermal stress dynamics and regional drivers across the Arabian Peninsula during 1990–2022 from observations</em>, led by Waheed Ullah (open access; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.70640">doi:10.1002/joc.70640</a>). Using 33 years of observations from 75 stations, we find thermal stress intensifying in every season — fastest in summer, at 0.64 °C per decade — with about five more strong-heat-stress days per decade. Information-flow analysis shows that air temperature is the dominant driver in most regions and seasons, while the roles of wind and radiant temperature differ from region to region, pointing to region-specific adaptation.</p>

    <p><strong>Soil moisture and heat extremes in <em>Climate Dynamics</em>.</strong> <em>Observed soil-moisture initialization reveals strong land-surface control on air-temperature extremes in Southeast China</em>, led by Kenny Lim Kam Sian (<em>Clim. Dyn.</em> 64, 469; <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00382-026-08426-3">doi:10.1007/s00382-026-08426-3</a>). Paired regional-climate experiments for 2000–2018 — one with soil moisture constrained daily by satellite observations, one with only its climatology, under identical circulation — isolate what soil moisture alone does to heat. Southeast China emerges as a coupling hotspot: soil-moisture anomalies raise mean summer temperature by up to about 0.5 °C and add up to three heatwave days a year, acting most strongly in the hottest tail. During the 2013 heatwave, dry soils shifted surface energy from latent to sensible heat, deepening the boundary layer and warming the near-surface air by about 0.2 °C.</p>

      <p><strong>CLeaR 2026.</strong> Our paper <em>Causal importance for physics-informed machine learning</em> (with Thomas Mortier, Cas Decancq and Diego Miralles) appeared in the <em>Proceedings of Machine Learning Research</em> (vol. 323) for the Conference on Causal Learning and Reasoning. We introduce a <em>Causal Importance Score</em> (CIS): conditioner-aware, time-varying information flow is distilled into indices of mediation, moderation, confounding and causal sufficiency, which then act as a causal prior that regularises a neural network — so the model's use of its inputs is steered towards the causal structure in the data rather than left to the training set alone.</p>

      <p><strong>Nature Communications: dry soil, hotter summers — the hotspots are moving north.</strong> <em>Divergent regional responses of soil moisture–air temperature coupling under future climate scenarios</em> is published (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74040-w">doi:10.1038/s41467-026-74040-w</a>). Moist soil acts as the land's cooling system: evaporating water cools the air, and when the soil dries out that cooling stops and heat builds faster. Using the soil moisture–temperature coupling metric Π of Miralles et al. (2012) across 11 CMIP6 climate models, we mapped where this soil–heat link will weaken or strengthen by 2100. Under high emissions, today's hotspots in the Sahel, India, the western US and central-east China fade, while new ones emerge over northern Europe, Canada, the northern US and humid regions such as the Amazon — so more of northern Europe may face summers in which droughts and heatwaves feed each other, while the Mediterranean remains a hotspot. If emissions are kept low, the link strengthens mostly where it already is instead of moving north.</p>
