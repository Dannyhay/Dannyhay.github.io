      <p><strong>Nature Communications: dry soil, hotter summers — the hotspots are moving north.</strong> <em>Divergent regional responses of soil moisture–air temperature coupling under future climate scenarios</em> is published (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74040-w">doi:10.1038/s41467-026-74040-w</a>). Moist soil acts as the land's cooling system: evaporating water cools the air, and when the soil dries out that cooling stops and heat builds faster. Using the soil moisture–temperature coupling metric Π of Miralles et al. (2012) across 11 CMIP6 climate models, we mapped where this soil–heat link will weaken or strengthen by 2100. Under high emissions, today's hotspots in the Sahel, India, the western US and central-east China fade, while new ones emerge over northern Europe, Canada, the northern US and humid regions such as the Amazon — so more of northern Europe may face summers in which droughts and heatwaves feed each other, while the Mediterranean remains a hotspot. If emissions are kept low, the link strengthens mostly where it already is instead of moving north.</p>
